Why does hot melt glue string, and how do I stop it?

Stringing ("angel hair") usually means the glue is cutting off poorly at the nozzle: the nozzle is too far from the work, the glue is too cool and viscous, the substrate is cold, the nozzle tip is damaged or charred, solenoid air pressure is low, or a draft is blowing on the bead.

Sources: H.B. Fuller Hot Melt Troubleshooting Guide; Henkel, "Preventing stringing in hot melt packaging adhesives"

Common follow-up questions

What is "angel hair" in hot melt gluing?

It is the thin strands of hot melt pulled away from the nozzle when the glue does not separate cleanly. The strands float through the air and land on equipment and products.

How close should the nozzle be to the board?

H.B. Fuller recommends that the applicator is not too far from the substrate, ideally 1/4 inch (about 6 mm).

Can a worn nozzle cause stringing?

Yes. Henkel notes that damage or char on the nozzle tip prevents the valve from operating properly, which leads to poor cut-off.
