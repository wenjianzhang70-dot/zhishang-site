Hot Glue Not Sticking? Causes and Fixes

Weak hot melt bonds usually come from glue that is too cool to wet the surface, too little glue, poor pressing, a hard-to-bond surface (film-laminated or UV-coated paper), or a cold substrate that sets the glue too early. Fix temperature, glue amount and pressing first, then check whether the adhesive suits the material. [1][2][3]

Quick test (from LD Davis, for wraps): pull a glued turn-in apart; paper fiber tearing means good adhesion, a clean peel means a glue or pressing issue. [4]

How this relates to Zhishang machines: Our hot-glue and cold-glue dispensing machines apply glue automatically along a set path. The brochure states one in-line machine "saves two people's gluing work." Adhesive choice and pressing still need to be matched to your materials.

Step-by-step: check in this order

  1. Verify the glue temperature where it is applied — tank, hose and nozzle — against the adhesive's recommended range.
  2. Check the glue amount on a test box: too thin a film cannot wet the wrap properly.
  3. Check the pressing: firm pressure, applied while the glue is still open, held long enough.
  4. Bring cold board and wrap to workshop temperature so the glue does not set on contact.
  5. Identify the surface: film-laminated or UV-coated wraps are hard to bond — trial a grade made for coated stock.
  6. Compare your assembly time with the glue's open time; if you are slower, shorten the path or pick a longer-open-time grade.
