Hot Melt Nozzle Clogging, Charring and Smoking

Clogged nozzles are usually caused by char: glue degraded by heat, oxygen, contamination and time. Dust and debris in the tank, long idle periods at full temperature and a near-empty tank all speed it up. Smoking or darkening glue is a sign of overheating or oxidised glue. Keep the tank covered and at least half full, use standby (setback) temperature when idle, filter, and replace charred glue. [1][2][3]

Sources: H.B. Fuller / Adhemelt Hot Melt Preventative Maintenance (PDF)；H.B. Fuller Packaging Adhesive Best Practices (PDF)；H.B. Fuller Hot Melt Troubleshooting Guide (PDF)

Step-by-step: check in this order

  1. If the glue has darkened or smokes, stop and replace the degraded glue — do not keep topping up over char.
  2. Clean or replace the tank and inline filters; most char starts as contamination here.
  3. Inspect the nozzle and hoses for carbon build-up; clean or replace the affected parts.
  4. Keep the tank covered and at least half full to limit contact with air and oxidation.
  5. Use the standby (setback) temperature whenever the line idles — do not hold the glue at full temperature for hours.
  6. Put filters and nozzles on a fixed inspection schedule instead of waiting for the next blockage.
